# Assets

**Assets** is the register of what the business owns that lasts more than a year: computers, equipment, furniture, tools, a vehicle you claim with a logbook. Tiny Tally works out each year's depreciation by your country's rules and puts it in your books.

![The asset register in the demo books](/shots/assets.webp)

## Adding an asset

Record the purchase as usual, in **Equipment** or an asset category. The register lists purchases from this year that aren't in it yet: **Add to register** fills in the date, the cost (before GST, if you're registered) and the category. Or use **Add asset** for anything else.

Choose how it's depreciated:

- **Australia**: the **instant asset write-off** for assets under the year's limit, the **small business pool** (15% the first year, 30% after) for the rest, or **diminishing value** or **prime cost** over the ATO's effective life if you don't use the small business rules.
- **New Zealand**: a **low-value asset** under the limit is written off at once. Otherwise **diminishing value** or **straight line** at IRD's rate for the asset.
- **United States**: **section 179 / bonus / de minimis** expenses it at once. Otherwise **MACRS 5-year** (computers, vehicles) or **7-year** (furniture, most equipment), or **straight line**.

If you use it privately too, set the **business use**: only that share is claimed.

Something bought before you started these books? Choose **Bought before these books** and enter its value for tax at the start of a year, from last year's return.

## Each year

Pick the year and check the figures, then **Post depreciation**. That adds one journal, dated the last day of the year:

- The year an asset is bought, its cost moves from the expense category to **Business Assets** (unless it's written off at once).
- The depreciation goes to **Depreciation**, on your tax return's depreciation line, against **Accumulated depreciation** on the balance sheet. The private share goes to **Owner Drawings**.

Change an asset later and the year shows **Changed since it was posted**: post it again. Post the years in order.

## Selling or scrapping

Choose **Sold or scrapped…** on the asset. The money you got is recorded as income in **Asset Sales**, with GST if you're registered. Posting that year takes the asset off the books and works out the gain or loss against the value left: a gain is income on your return (US: Form 4797, not Schedule C), a loss is a deduction. New Zealand has no depreciation in the year an asset is sold.
