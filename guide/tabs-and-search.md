# Tabs, windows, search and calculator

## Tabs

Look at several things at once in the same window: an invoice, the customer's statement and the bank register, say.

![Three tabs on the same books](/shots/tabs.webp)

- **New Tab**: <kbd>⌘T</kbd>, the **+** in the tab strip, or a double-click on an empty part of the strip.
- **Open a link in a new tab**: <kbd>⌘</kbd>-click it (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>-click on Windows and Linux), or middle-click. Add <kbd>⇧</kbd> to open it behind the current tab.
- **Switch**: click a tab, press <kbd>⌘1</kbd> to <kbd>⌘9</kbd>, or <kbd>⇧⌘]</kbd> and <kbd>⇧⌘[</kbd> for the next and previous.
- **Close**: <kbd>⌘W</kbd>, the × on the tab, or middle-click it.
- **Reorder**: drag a tab along the strip.
- Right-click a tab for **Close Other Tabs** and **Move Tab to New Window**.

Each tab remembers where it's been, so Back and Forward (<kbd>⌘[</kbd> and <kbd>⌘]</kbd>) work per tab. A tab you come back to is as you left it, filters and all. Your tabs are remembered for each set of books.

## Windows

**New Window** (<kbd>⌘N</kbd>) opens another window on the same books, at the page you're on. It's handy across two screens. You can also drag a tab out of the strip to give it its own window.

Every tab and window on the same books stays up to date: record a payment in one and the invoice list in another changes too.

To work on a different set of books at the same time, use **Open Books…** (<kbd>⌘O</kbd>). Each set of books opens in its own window. Give each one its own **Colour** in Settings so you can tell them apart.

## Search {#search}

<kbd>⌘K</kbd> (or click **Search** at the top of the window) finds:

- pages and actions, like *New invoice* or *Rules*
- transactions, invoices, quotes, credit notes and bills, by description, number, contact, reference or total
- contacts, accounts and categories

![Searching for "office"](/shots/search.webp)

Type an amount, like `89.99` or `$1,234.50`, to find that exact amount.

The last result, *All transactions matching…*, opens the Transactions page with that search.

## Calculator {#calculator}

**Go › Calculator** (<kbd>⇧⌘C</kbd>, <kbd>Ctrl Shift C</kbd> on Windows and Linux) opens a calculator over whatever you're doing, even an open expense or invoice.

- Type a sum, such as `1,250 + 10%` or `(84.50 + 12) × 3`, or click the keys. The answer shows as you type; <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>=</kbd> works it out. Keep typing an operator to carry on from the answer.
- `+ 10%` adds ten percent and `− 10%` takes it off, as on a desk calculator. `× 10%` is ten percent of the amount.
- <kbd>⌘C</kbd> with nothing selected copies the answer, ready to paste into an amount. Paste in amounts from anywhere: currency signs and commas are dropped, and `(1,234.50)` comes in as −1,234.50.
- Your last few sums are listed underneath. Click one to use its answer.
- Drag it by its title to move it out of the way. It opens there next time; double-click the title to put it back in the middle.
- <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes it, and you're back where you were typing.

### Sums in amount fields {#sums-in-fields}

In any amount or number field, type a number, then <kbd>+</kbd>, <kbd>−</kbd>, <kbd>*</kbd> or <kbd>/</kbd>. It works on a number already there too, at the end of it or with it all selected (as when you tab into the field); type a digit instead and it replaces the number as usual. A tape opens under the field with your number on the first line and the operator on the next, set out like a sum on paper. Type the next number, another operator for another line, and so on, with the total underneath.

- <kbd>Enter</kbd> (or <kbd>=</kbd>) puts the total in the field. <kbd>Tab</kbd> does too, and moves on. <kbd>Esc</kbd> leaves the field as it was.
- Each line works on the total so far, top to bottom, as an adding machine does: 100 + 20 × 2 is 240.
- <kbd>%</kbd> after a number makes it a percentage: + 10% adds ten percent of the total so far.
- <kbd>Backspace</kbd> on an empty line takes it away. Typing a different operator on an empty line swaps it.
- A minus in an empty field is still just a negative number. To type a negative over a selected number, delete it first.
